# Learning the calibrated clock

[Repository overview](../README.md) · [Scientific route](../science/README.md)

The selected educational anchor is **Landi, Kewming, Mitchison and Potts, _Current Fluctuations in Open Quantum Systems: Bridging the Gap Between Quantum Continuous Measurements and Full Counting Statistics_, PRX Quantum 5, 020201 (2024)**. The [source bridge](anchor_bridge.md) fixes the published version, gives a short section route and translates its counting conventions.

The [30-milestone map](milestones.md) follows this project's own question: how source fluctuations become timing error when the source speed must be learned with a finite temporary reference. It connects background objects to the converse, finite protocol, thermal application, classical comparison and stability limits.

Each milestone identifies a scientific understanding target, its prerequisite concepts and an authoritative route. The map is a reader-independent guide; it makes no claim about a particular reader's progress.

Keep four distinctions visible throughout the route:

- The signed count includes reverse events, and its noise includes correlations.
- A known-current accuracy coefficient does not price learning the actual current.
- Reuse amortizes calibration expense without averaging away the shared scale error.
- The complete modeled activity comparison is conditional on the declared reference certification, detector and memory resources; it is not a complete laboratory-cost theorem.

The source supplies the open-system and current-fluctuation toolkit. The [related-work account](../references/RELATED_WORK.md) credits the inherited calibration and clock constructions, while the project science supplies the additional minimax and resource/reuse arguments.
